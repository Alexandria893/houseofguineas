# House of Guineas — Growth Strategy

Private reference doc (repo root, not published by Hugo). Companion to `MARKETING.md`,
which covers the website changes and the Google Business Profile playbook.

**Goal:** be the default — first and only — resource for exotic pet care in SF & the
Peninsula, with a consistently full in-home care schedule.

**Grounding (July 2026):** YTD revenue ≈ $23.4k vs. expenses ≈ $25.3k (roughly
break-even; wages are ~65% of revenue; $0 spent on advertising). In the last ~5 weeks
the business line logged **38 missed calls**, 12 with voicemails, and at least **7 were
new-client inquiries** — boarding and multi-day in-home jobs, plus one vet-referred
medical-care client. Several callers tried 3–5 times without reaching anyone.

The conclusion: demand already exists. The bottleneck is **capturing** it, then
**compounding** it. Everything below is ordered by leverage.

---

## 1. Stop the leak — answer every inquiry (do this first)

Industry rule of thumb: most callers who hit voicemail never call back; the sitter who
responds first wins the booking. You cannot answer the phone mid-visit, so build a
system that responds even when you can't:

1. **Auto-text-back on every missed call** (set up in the phone system):
   > "Hi, this is Alexandria at House of Guineas 🐾 I'm likely mid-visit with a pet
   > right now. Text me your pet's species + the dates you need and I'll reply within
   > the hour — or book directly at houseofguineas.com/book."
   This single change converts hang-ups (the majority of missed calls) into text
   conversations.
2. **15-minute response target during business hours** for texts/voicemails; same-day
   for everything else. Speed-to-lead is the highest-ROI "marketing" there is.
3. **Voicemail greeting that routes to text:** "…the fastest way to reach me is by
   text to this number, or book at houseofguineas.com/book."
4. **A follow-up task for every inquiry, same day.** No inquiry is closed until it's
   booked, declined, or has had 3 touches (day 0, day 2, day 5). A trip-planner who
   doesn't hear back books elsewhere within a day.
5. **Track it weekly:** inquiries → responded within 15 min → meet-and-greets →
   booked. That conversion rate is the number to move before spending anything on
   new demand.

## 2. Build the moat — the "medical-grade" positioning no one else can copy

Generic sitters can feed a rabbit. They cannot give injections, syringe-feed a
GI-stasis-prone guinea pig, or verify UVB and basking gradients. That's the wedge that
makes House of Guineas *the only* option, not just the first one:

1. **Vet-referral channel (highest quality leads that exist).** A recent injection-care
   client came from a vet clinic referral — proof the channel works. Formalize it:
   - List every exotic/avian vet from SF through Redwood City (the site already has a
     directory page — use it as the call list).
   - Visit each in person with cards and a one-page "post-op & medication care" flyer:
     injections, sub-Q fluids, syringe feeding, med schedules, recovery monitoring.
   - The pitch to the clinic: "When you discharge an exotic patient whose owner can't
     do injections or travels during recovery, we're your hands at home." Vet front
     desks answer "who can care for my bunny?" every single day.
   - Follow up quarterly; report back ("your referral's slider finished her injection
     course beautifully") so referring becomes rewarding.
2. **Add a "Medical & Recovery Care" service page** (`/exotic-pet-medication-injection-care/`):
   injections, fluids, critical care feeding, post-surgical monitoring — priced at a
   premium (this is skilled labor; do not price it like a feeding visit).
3. **Say the credentials everywhere, first.** Exotic vet assistant, Bay Area Bird &
   Exotics Hospital, Pet CPR/First Aid, bonded & insured, PSI member. Every page,
   every profile, every reply. This is why someone pays $85/visit instead of $25 on
   Rover — make the comparison easy.

## 3. Make it consistent — recurring revenue as the base layer

"Swamped" from vacation sitting alone is seasonal (July and December spike; February
is quiet). Consistency comes from routine clients:

1. **Set a target: 6–8 routine clients** (2–3 visits/week). At current plan pricing
   that's roughly $4–7k/month of predictable revenue before any travel care — enough
   to cover current payroll on its own.
2. **Convert existing travel clients.** Every travel client has already trusted you in
   their home. At the end of each travel booking, offer a routine plan: "Want me to
   keep up [pet]'s cage deep-cleans and weigh-ins weekly? Most of my routine clients
   started exactly this way." One conversion per month compounds fast.
3. **Convert boarding inquiries you can't take.** When boarding is full (holidays),
   offer in-home visits as the alternative instead of a plain "no" — in-home has no
   capacity ceiling the way your boarding space does.
4. **Waitlist + rebooking cadence.** After every travel job: "Want me to pencil in
   your next trip's dates now? Holiday weeks fill 6–8 weeks out." Book the next job
   before the current one ends.

## 4. Compound demand — the flywheel that makes you "the first" answer

The MARKETING.md playbook (GBP, reviews, weekly posts, Q&A seeding) is the engine for
Google/Maps visibility — **execute it fully; it's the single biggest discovery
channel and it's free.** On top of it:

1. **Reviews are the whole ballgame locally.** Ask every single client, twice (the
   templates are in MARKETING.md). Target: 2–4 new Google reviews/month. The profile
   with 40 five-star exotic-specific reviews *is* "the first and only resource" as far
   as Google is concerned.
2. **Referral partner network.** A cat sitter already refers clients — make that
   systematic. Every dog/cat sitter, walker, and Rover top-sitter in SF-Peninsula gets
   the same offer: "Send me the guinea pigs, rabbits, reptiles and birds you don't
   want; I send you every dog and cat inquiry I get." You're not their competitor —
   you're their answer to a client they'd otherwise disappoint. Aim for 10 active
   partners; check in monthly.
3. **Rescues, societies, and stores.** House Rabbit Society (existing volunteer
   connection), guinea pig/rat rescues, reptile societies, exotic-friendly pet stores:
   offer a first-visit discount for adopters, a care talk, or sponsored adoption-kit
   flyers. Adopters are brand-new owners with zero existing sitter relationship.
4. **Own the answers.** The blog already targets care questions — keep one post/month
   aimed at what owners actually ask (the voicemails are a topic goldmine: "boarding
   vs. in-home for rabbits," "how to find a sitter who can medicate your pet",
   "what does exotic pet boarding cost in SF"). Each post is bait for the exact person
   who becomes a client.
5. **A simple monthly email** to every past client and meet-and-greet: one care tip,
   one availability note ("August boarding: 2 spots left"), one review/referral ask.
   Past clients are the cheapest bookings you'll ever get; scarcity drives early
   booking.

## 5. Protect the reputation — service recovery

At this size, one angry review costs more than ten new clients. When a dispute
surfaces (e.g., a surprise fee or penalty a client didn't expect):

- Call back same-day, hear them out, and err on the side of waiving one-time charges
  in exchange for goodwill.
- Then fix the root cause: put fees/penalties in writing at booking (the booking form
  and confirmation text are the place), so "surprising" never happens twice.

## 6. Economics — what "swamped" needs to look like on paper

- Wages currently consume ~65% of revenue and the business is at break-even. Growth
  must come with **margin**, not just volume:
  - Fill *your own* schedule first (highest margin), staff second.
  - Route staff to standard visits; keep medical/premium visits (and their pricing)
    with the most qualified person.
  - Revisit prices annually and any time you're >80% booked — being swamped at
    break-even prices is a treadmill, not a business. When demand exceeds supply,
    raise prices before adding heads.
- Keep advertising at $0 until the capture system (Section 1) and GBP are done. Paid
  ads on top of a leaky funnel buys more missed calls. Once response rate and reviews
  are solid, a small Google Ads test on "exotic pet sitter san francisco" / "rabbit
  boarding bay area" is the first paid experiment.

## The operating rhythm (what "consistently" actually means)

**Daily (15 min):** respond to every inquiry same-day; log each one with a follow-up
task; confirm tomorrow's visits.

**Weekly (1 hour):** publish the GBP post (calendar in MARKETING.md); send review
requests to the week's finished clients; run follow-ups on open inquiries (day-2 /
day-5 touches); check the numbers below.

**Monthly (half day):** one blog post; the client email; one partner-network touch
(a vet visit, a sitter coffee, a rescue check-in); refresh GBP photos.

**Scoreboard (5 numbers, tracked weekly):**
1. Inquiries received / % responded within 15 min
2. Meet-and-greets booked
3. New bookings (and $ value)
4. Routine-care clients active (target: 6–8)
5. Google reviews total (target: +2–4/month)

**Order of operations:** Section 1 this week → GBP playbook + review engine over the
next 30 days → vet-referral circuit and partner network over the next 90 → then
evaluate paid ads. Demand is already knocking; the strategy is to open the door every
time, give Google proof you're the best answer, and convert one-time trips into
weekly routines.
