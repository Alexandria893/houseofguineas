## Why Guinea Pigs Make Great Pets

Guinea pigs are one of the most rewarding small animals you can keep. They're social, vocal, and full of personality — every guinea pig owner knows the joy of hearing their pigs wheek at the sound of the fridge opening. With a lifespan of 5 to 7 years, they're longer-lived than hamsters or gerbils, which means you genuinely build a bond with them over time.

They're also gentle enough for families with children, provided kids are taught proper handling. But I want to be honest: guinea pigs are not a starter pet. They need daily fresh food, regular cage cleaning, veterinary care from an exotic-trained vet, and a companion of their own species. If you're ready for that commitment, they'll reward you with years of personality and affection.

## Housing & Cage Setup

The single biggest mistake new guinea pig owners make is buying a cage that's too small. Those pet store cages marketed for guinea pigs are almost always inadequate. For a pair of guinea pigs, you need a minimum of **10.5 square feet** of continuous floor space — and bigger is always better.

**C&C (cubes and coroplast) cages** are the gold standard in the guinea pig community. They're affordable, customizable, and give your pigs the space they need to run, popcorn, and establish their own territories. You can find quality cage options on our [small mammal resources page](/small-mammal-resources), including Critter Colony cages.

**Inside the cage, you'll need:**
* At least two hides (one per pig, plus an extra)
* A hay rack or hay bag to keep timothy hay off the floor
* A water bottle with a sipper tube (check it daily for clogs)
* A heavy ceramic food bowl for pellets and vegetables
* Fleece tunnels or bridges for enrichment

For a complete walkthrough of setting up your first cage, see our [cage setup guide](/post/guinea-pig-cage-setup).

## Bedding: The Foundation of Guinea Pig Health

Bedding might seem like an afterthought, but it's one of the most critical factors in your guinea pig's health. Your pigs sit on their bedding 24 hours a day — if it traps moisture, harbors bacteria, or creates dust, you're looking at real health consequences: **bumblefoot** (painful foot sores), **upper respiratory infections** from ammonia and dust, and chronic skin irritation.

The three main bedding options are paper bedding, wood shavings, and fleece liners. Each has trade-offs, and I've written a full breakdown in [fleece liners vs disposable bedding](/post/guinea-pig-fleece-liners-vs-disposable-bedding). But here's the short version:

* **Paper bedding** (Carefresh) — decent absorption but saturates quickly, creates dust, and costs $20-40/month
* **Wood shavings** — cheap but risky; pine and cedar contain phenols that damage respiratory tissue; only kiln-dried aspen is considered safe
* **Fleece liners** — reusable, dust-free, and dry on the surface when properly designed

## Diet & Nutrition

A healthy guinea pig diet is simpler than you'd think, but getting it wrong can cause serious problems.

**The essentials:**
* **Timothy hay — 80% of the diet, unlimited.** Hay keeps the digestive system moving and wears down continuously growing teeth. It should be available at all times.
* **Guinea pig pellets** — a small, measured amount daily. Choose pellets fortified with vitamin C (not rabbit pellets, which lack it). [Small Pet Select](/small-mammal-resources) is my go-to brand for both hay and pellets.
* **Fresh vegetables daily** — bell peppers (especially red and yellow — highest in vitamin C), romaine lettuce, cilantro, and leafy greens. Introduce new vegetables slowly to avoid digestive upset.
* **Fresh water** — always available, changed daily.

**A critical note on vitamin C:** Guinea pigs, like humans, cannot produce their own vitamin C. A deficiency leads to scurvy — symptoms include swollen joints, lethargy, rough coat, and reluctance to move. A diet rich in bell peppers and supplemented with quality pellets usually provides enough, but talk to your vet if you're unsure.

## Health: What to Watch For

Guinea pigs are prey animals, which means they instinctively hide signs of illness. By the time a guinea pig looks visibly sick, the problem may already be advanced. Knowing the early warning signs can save your pig's life.

**The 5 key health concerns to watch for:**

1. **Upper respiratory infections (URIs)** — wheezing, clicking, nasal discharge, lethargy. This is an emergency.
2. **GI stasis** — not eating, small or absent droppings, hunched posture. If your pig hasn't eaten in 12 hours, see a vet immediately.
3. **Bumblefoot** — swollen, red, or scabbed foot pads, often caused by wet or rough bedding.
4. **Dental problems** — drooling, dropping food, weight loss. Guinea pig teeth grow continuously and can develop painful spurs.
5. **General lethargy** — a guinea pig that's sitting puffed up, not responding to food, or hiding more than usual needs attention.

**Weigh your guinea pigs weekly.** A kitchen scale is one of the best diagnostic tools you can own. A loss of 50+ grams in a week is a red flag.

## Socialization & Enrichment

Guinea pigs are herd animals. They need at least one companion of their own species — a lone guinea pig, no matter how much human attention they get, is a stressed guinea pig. Same-sex pairs or small groups work best. If you have a boar (male), neutering allows him to live with sows, but two bonded boars can also do well together.

**Enrichment ideas:**
* **Floor time** — let your pigs explore a pig-proofed room or playpen daily. Mine go wild for it.
* **Tunnels and hides** — guinea pigs love running through tunnels and having multiple hiding spots.
* **Foraging opportunities** — scatter pellets in hay, hide vegetables in paper bags, or use a snuffle mat.
* **Gentle handling** — always support your guinea pig's full body with both hands. Let them come to you rather than chasing them around the cage. Short, positive handling sessions build trust over time.

Milo took three weeks before he'd willingly climb into my hands. Kai was confident from day one. Every pig is different — patience is everything.

## Thinking About Adopting?

If this guide has you thinking about bringing guinea pigs into your life, please adopt rather than shop. Guinea pig rescues are full of healthy, social animals that need homes. Check your local shelters and rescues first — many even have bonded pairs available, which means the hard work of introductions is already done.

**What to expect the first week:**
* Your new pigs will be scared. That's normal.
* Keep the environment quiet, offer plenty of hay and hides, and resist the urge to handle them constantly.
* Let them settle in for 2-3 days before starting short handling sessions.
* Watch for signs of stress or illness during the transition.
