Finding a bird sitter in San Francisco who truly understands birds is harder than it should be. Most pet sitters either don't take birds at all or treat them like a fish — a quick feed-and-fly, with no real sense of what's normal versus what's an emergency. Birds are subtle, and they hide illness until it's nearly too late — so the person looking after yours needs to know how to read those quiet signals. That's exactly why House of Guineas is here.

Our veterinary-informed team, with backgrounds spanning wildlife rehabilitation to exotic veterinary hospital work, brings a clinical lens to every bird visit. Our founder, Alexandria, previously worked as an exotic veterinary assistant at [Bay Area Bird and Exotics Hospital](https://www.birdandexoticsvet.com), where birds were a major part of the caseload — from cockatiel emergencies to large parrot wellness exams — and that standard carries through everything we do: every visit includes proper feeding, husbandry, and careful observation, not just "make sure the bird is alive."

## What's Included in a Bird Visit

- **Fresh chop preparation** — washed, sorted, and portioned to your bird's diet. Pellet/chop ratio kept consistent with your routine.
- **Pellet measurement** — your bird gets exactly the amount you specified, carefully measured at every visit.
- **Water tower or bowl change** — fresh daily, with the dish cleaned thoroughly each visit.
- **Full cage cleaning** as scheduled — paper change, grate cleaning, perch wipe-down. Everything goes back exactly where your bird expects it, since big rearrangements are stressful for birds — we keep their world familiar while you're away.
- **Out-of-cage time** for birds who already have established free-flight or perch time with their pet parents. We stick faithfully to the routine your bird already knows.
- **Photo and video updates** from every visit, plus written notes on appetite, droppings, vocalization patterns, and overall behavior.

If your bird is on medication — oral suspensions, topical applications, or nebulizer treatments — we can administer it, gently and right on schedule.

## Free-Flight Handling

Some pet parents have trained their birds for free-flight time at home. For everyone's safety, we manage free-flight only for birds who are already trained for it — and when it's part of your bird's normal routine, we handle it with care: doors and windows secured, ceiling fans off, hazards cleared, recall practiced. The protocol matches what you do at home, not a cookie-cutter approach.

For birds who are clipped or who don't free-fly, we focus on cage-side enrichment, target training (if your bird already knows it), and conversation/whistling time, just the way your bird likes it.

## Recognizing Illness Signs

Birds hide illness — by the time symptoms are obvious, it's often urgent. That's why our team keeps a practiced eye out for:

- Fluffed-up posture lasting more than an hour while the bird is at rest
- Tail bobbing while breathing (indicates respiratory effort)
- Open-mouth breathing
- Discharge from nostrils or eyes
- Sitting on the cage floor when not sleeping
- Refusing food for more than 12–24 hours
- Significant changes in droppings — color, consistency, or near-zero output
- Unusual quiet or sudden change in vocalization patterns
- Visible blood

If we see any of these, we contact you immediately and recommend whether your bird needs to be seen. We know the local avian vet landscape — Bay Area Bird and Exotics Hospital in San Francisco is often the first call; Medical Center for Birds in Oakley is the go-to for complex cases.
